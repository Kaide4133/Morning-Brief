<article class='mainArticle'>
<p>By Wen-Yee Lee</p><p>TAIPEI, Oct 7 (Reuters) – A Taiwan union representing workers at Micron Technology said on Wednesday it had secured authorisation from its members to strike over a dispute concerning the company’s bonus scheme, escalating a labour conflict at the ​chipmaker’s largest manufacturing hub.</p><p>Micron is the latest chipmaker to face labour unrest as ‌employees seek a larger share of the AI, which has propelled leading memory producers to record profits and margins.</p><p>The union, which represents workers at Micron’s operations in the northern city of Taoyuan, said 1,994 members, or 99% of those casting ballots, voted in favour of a strike. It said details of any strike, ‌including ​its timing, were still under discussion.</p><p>Taiwan is a crucial production ⁠centre for Micron’s DRAM and ⁠high-bandwidth memory (HBM) chips, key components in AI servers. A labour action could amplify supply concerns in an already tight memory-chip market.</p><p>The union said it still hoped the company would propose a concrete profit-sharing proposal for employees before it resorted to a strike. It is ​encouraging members to take a day off on October 19 to join a rally in Taipei, which it said would not be a strike.</p><p>Micron employs about 15,000 people in ⁠Taiwan, with separate unions representing workers at its Taoyuan ⁠and Taichung operations.</p><p>Mediation with the Taoyuan union ended without agreement in ​September, while the Taichung union remains in negotiations.</p><p>Together, the two unions represent more than 80% of ​Micron’s Taiwan workforce.</p><p>Micron in September announced fiscal 2026 rewards for more than 60,000 employees ‌globally following what it described as an “extraordinary year” for the company. That included Taiwan employees receiving a T$1 million ($31,386) cash bonus.</p><p>However, the Taoyuan union said the package failed to address its demand for a permanent profit-sharing mechanism and criticised Micron for announcing them while labour mediation ⁠was underway without first reaching an agreement with the union.</p><p>Micron, which has not linked its fiscal 2026 rewards to the labour dispute in Taiwan, said on Wednesday it took employees’ concerns ⁠seriously and remained committed to ‌engaging with its unions in good faith.</p><p>“We will continue to work ⁠through the established dialogue and mediation processes,” the company said, adding ​that it ‌would hold a further mediation session with the Taichung union ​later this month ⁠to work towards a constructive solution.</p><p>Taiwan’s labour ministry said in a statement to Reuters that it respected the union’s strike vote and urged Micron to consider workers’ concerns, put forward concrete proposals and negotiate with the union.</p><p>Shares in Micron were down more than 2% in US premarket trading on Wednesday.</p><p>($1 = 31.8610 Taiwan dollars)</p><p>(Reporting by Wen-Yee Lee; Writing by Ben Blanchard; Editing by Miyoung ​Kim and Kim Coghill)</p><div class="clear"></div><footer class="entry-footer">
